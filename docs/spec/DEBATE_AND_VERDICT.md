## Decision: keep one precise useful contract

**ScopeWatch identifies an authenticated workload exceeding its operator's predeclared allowance of unique native ALLOW permission decisions across completed sessions, creates a grounded review record through Guild, and verifies an operator-approved restriction of subsequent matching operations while an approved workload still returns its expected result through the same credential.**

The exact core is an **asynchronous approval-event circuit breaker with reviewed selective response**. Calling it quota monitoring is correct. Its useful product outcome is accountable restriction with verified continuity of approved work. Its detector does not establish task drift, successful data access, malicious intent, theft or prompt injection.

The operator has two real Guild-hosted workloads in one owned synthetic repository integration. Support triage has a small allowance; an explicitly preapproved release-review job has a larger one. The busier bulk workload must have higher raw volume yet remain within its own allowance. The support workload's repeated short completed sessions collectively exceed its allowance. ClickHouse selects support from the verified candidate population. One real investigator reads the pinned operator manifest, explains the contrast and evidence gaps, and creates an actual incident. The operator reviews and applies the actual target rule. A later matching support operation produces native policy denial; a fresh bulk call returns a reserved synthetic fixture that the team inspects in the real tool result.

That final pair is the centerpiece: **a particular later operation was refused, and a particular approved operation still worked**. Neither a policy-created response nor an ALLOW badge completes the story.

Use one fixed set of fixture counts after the account proof establishes the unit. The source briefs contain different illustrative counts and windows; they are examples, not separate product requirements. One possible predeclared case is support 30 against 20 and bulk 40 against 60 in the same rolling ten-minute interval. These numbers are planned lab parameters, never observed results or production recommendations. Do not change a threshold after seeing the behavior or assume one permission event equals one ticket.

## Exact policy and evidence contract

1. **Trusted declaration:** pin one operator-owned manifest version/hash before launching the scenario. It binds authenticated installed agent/workspace identity, shared credential, one exact selected operation, allowance and interval. The investigator cannot edit it. Task names, ticket text and agent-authored “approved” labels confer no authority.
2. **Native unit and time:** count unique native security decisions whose actual decision is ALLOW and operation matches the selected family. Use the established native event record-creation clock in a declared half-open rolling interval. Show collector observation separately. Record creation is not external service execution or delivery time. If that clock is unavailable, choose and disclose a different observation-time contract before proceeding; never silently substitute it.
3. **Complete coverage:** enumerate the finite controller-launched lab sessions and retain terminal-state, pagination and reconciliation evidence. Drain the relevant durable pages before advancing a cursor. Newest-first defaults, twenty-item pages, the thousand-item cap and exclusive `from_id` require a tested traversal. Unfinished pages or unresolved mappings are incomplete evidence, not compliant zero. A complete subset cannot justify a complete population claim.
4. **Canonical identity:** resolve the native event identity and counting-field conflicts before grouping. Matching repeated delivery counts once. Conflicting actor, credential, decision, operation or time requires reconciliation; exact distinct counting separately under two actors must not conceal the conflict. The authenticated platform/task graph and controller launch record establish workload identity.
5. **Analytical choice:** evaluate every manifest-bound, fully mapped and covered candidate in the authorized population. Apply each candidate's own allowance, return the breach and its contributing sessions, and save the actual result/query ID. Preserve excluded or unresolved evidence visibly. Do not use a dashboard filter, allegation or suspected actor as the policy predicate.
6. **Grounded investigation:** one hosted investigator reads the pinned manifest through a real repository tool, uses the bounded saved case, and creates an actual incident citing native sessions and the budget facts. It explains the approved bulk exception and uncertainty. A templated record may accomplish much of this; demonstrate the useful read/action without claiming indispensable model reasoning.
7. **Human action:** show the exact actor/workspace/credential/operation and any account-proven resource/method selectors before approval. Keep the actual applied native rule/identifier and approval observation. Changed actor, scope or evidence requires review of the revised concrete action. Approval in a local UI is local approval; native Guild policy provides enforcement.
8. **Two fresh outcomes:** after the policy change, require target native DENY with `POLICY_DENIED` for the same authenticated subject and no fresh successful result for that repeated matching operation. Require the separately attributable control, through the same verified credential, to return its expected reserved synthetic fixture in the actual tool result. Do not provide that expected marker to the model as its answer. A cached screen, agent's success claim, object existence, ALLOW, DONE or 2xx alone is insufficient.

Use the built-in GitHub integration and the selected `issues_get` policy operation where account proof supports it; its tool name is `github_issues_get`. The minimum containment scope is the verified agent/workspace/credential/operation. Repository/method conditions add precision only after actual enforcement proof. Resource selectors in a policy do not establish resource-level analytic event fields. The investigator needs only manifest-read and incident-create operations with real repository constraints. Remove the initial allow-all before claiming a restricted baseline; a successful targeted DENY alone does not establish least privilege elsewhere. [Guild policies](https://docs.guild.ai/platform/credential-policies), [GitHub mapping](https://docs.guild.ai/integrations/github), [events](https://docs.guild.ai/api-reference/sessions/fetch-session-events).

The trigger-key correction is accepted on the documented body, whose cached and normalized/indexed versions were compared. The index is reuse of the same capture, not independent fresh corroboration. Keep this workspace-capable key outside investigator input, tools and incident output; the controller uses the intended installed-agent allowlist. [API triggers](https://docs.guild.ai/platform/api-triggers), [scope comparison](rebuttal-guild.md).

## Outcomes strengthen evidence without changing the product

Only after the verified complete core loop, optionally run a focused native outcome experiment, without a clock cutoff. Attempt exact security-event-to-tool-task correlation using authenticated native identifiers. Test cardinality in both directions for the selected operation. Following task parents for actor attribution is permitted; multiplying descendants for result attribution is not. Nearest timestamps, sequence order, tool-name equality and model-authored labels are insufficient.

Matched rows may show actual available HTTP status, request bytes and response bytes as separately labeled context. Absent fields remain absent; ambiguous joins remain ambiguous; unmatched rows remain unmatched. `response_data` is projection-dependent and its absence does not imply failure. Even a perfect match does not turn an approval allowance into a successful-read, document or byte-leak allowance. The independently inspected fresh control result remains mandatory regardless of this experiment. [Native task schema](https://docs.guild.ai/api-reference/sessions/fetch-session-sub-tasks).

If exact matching cannot be supported, omit its stronger claim and keep the core receipt. Do not deploy a receipt server, evidence API, OAuth bridge, MCP integration or second remote service to rescue it. Keep required core readiness ahead of optional experiments.

## ClickHouse depth: earn scale without inflating the incident

Use raw native evidence and a bounded exact query initially. Plain MergeTree with explicit canonical identity handling is sufficient. Background replacement merges are not immediate uniqueness; insertion-time joins do not revise old rows when a right-side allowance changes; sums of minute counts do not establish arbitrary exact rolling windows. Pin the unchanged manifest in the live scenario. Defer rollups until raw correctness exists. [Exact aggregation](https://clickhouse.com/docs/reference/functions/aggregate-functions/uniqExact), [replacement behavior](https://clickhouse.com/docs/reference/engines/table-engines/mergetree-family/replacingmergetree), [MV joins](https://clickhouse.com/docs/concepts/features/materialized-views/incremental-materialized-view).

The scale panel is a separately labeled synthetic/replay experiment. Vary workload/session/credential identities, selected and unrelated operations, ALLOW/DENY/ERROR, windows, per-actor allowances, compliant busy cohorts, smaller breached cohorts, duplicates, late arrivals and mapping conflicts. Preserve expected and actual answers. Copying a picture into a million rows is a poor demonstration; repeating the same native ID must not inflate the active case.

Measure stored rows, distinct identities, candidate workload count, relevant-row fraction, actual rows read/memory and a disclosed timing sample with service/version/region and warm/cold conditions. Report p50/p95 only with that sample context. Save query IDs and output separately: query-log metadata alone is not result evidence. A roughly million-row target is optional. A modest diverse measured corpus is preferable to a large unverified badge. [Query log](https://clickhouse.com/docs/reference/system-tables/query_log).

Keep separate timings for completed-turn persistence/first observation, queryable insertion, SQL evaluation, hosted incident creation, approval/policy application and first subsequent denial. The event source persists after a turn; the detector cannot prevent the already-permitted excess in that turn. SQL milliseconds are not containment milliseconds.
