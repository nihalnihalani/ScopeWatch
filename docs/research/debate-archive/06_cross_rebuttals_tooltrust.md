# ToolTrust: round-two rebuttal and comparative judgment

Prepared 9 October 2026. Product/prize planning only; no new web research, implementation or obtained finding. Akash excluded. Responds to [the preliminary critique](../archive/source-tree/analysis/cyberdefense-2026-10-09/debate/05_devils_preliminary.md), [Code Vaccine](01_semgrep_advocate.md), and the latest ScopeWatch specification supplied for this round. The earlier GatewaySaidSo/PivotWatch drafts and their invented scores are not the operative alternatives.

## One concession

**ToolTrust's destination-transition hypothesis is not a lower-risk discovery bet than A's Guardian-supported JWT case.** It requires ordinary generated code to contain an interesting flaw, a supported stock CLI scan to find the actual source path, and controlled observation to establish the advertised recipient consequence. The stock source finding does not establish the later transition. The relevant inspected outbound rules are outside Guardian-default. If the generated connector is correct, the story cannot be made true by adapting the code to the detector. [Exact detection and attribution limits](../finding-feasibility.md).

## One cut

**Remove fleet ranking.** ToolTrust reviews one connector release; it should not invent several connectors merely to make ClickHouse appear necessary. Keep the same user and decision: “Has this connector version demonstrated its required delivery behavior without crossing its approved audience?”

## The no-finding core does not restart the build

Begin the connector-release card, approved-recipient contract, legitimate delivery checks and event evidence collection in parallel with the bounded scan. The contract comes from the intended feature requirements, independently of whether the scanner finds a defect. It records which delivery modes and audiences the owner actually requires and whether each required check has a result.

By the discovery cutoff, the same generated connector and the same review card continue down one of two branches:

- **Actual eligible finding:** attach the real source finding and observed consequence, review the correction, and retain the legitimate delivery checks.
- **No qualifying finding:** show a bounded acceptance review of the generated connector: required checks initially incomplete, actual results arriving, and the release evidence becoming complete. This is a connector-review prototype with no Semgrep finding-prize claim. It is not evidence that the app is comprehensively secure.

No replacement app, new audience model, intentionally flawed implementation or post-cutoff benchmark product is needed. The no-finding branch cannot promise the same dramatic failure/repair demonstration. Its honest value is whether the owner has sufficient measured evidence to approve the particular connector version. If an actual interesting JWT case is obtained elsewhere, it may justify choosing A, but that would be a decision between concepts rather than a hidden ToolTrust fallback.

Keep the discovery decision near 12:00 PM, require a useful card/query/hosted review by approximately 1:30 PM, and freeze the demonstration near 2:30 PM. The rest of the original 300-minute budget remains for validation, recording, access checks and submission.

## Why the case would be more than a generic SSRF flag

The advertised consequence must be a discrepancy between the recipient/audience the owner approved and the recipient/audience that actually received the synthetic report. The source flag alone is insufficient. A safe ordinary delivery remains the positive control, and the corrected release must retain it. A genuine destination transition may provide a particularly clear consequence if it is observed, but it remains optional evidence, not a prescribed generated defect.

There is no claim that recipient control, SSRF testing or release acceptance is a new category. The defensible subject is one actual AI-generated connector and the exact release decision supported by its evidence. If the finding is ordinary and the consequence uninteresting, product polish cannot manufacture Semgrep uniqueness. [Prior art and current-event limitations](../competition-and-eligibility.md).

## Meaningful ClickHouse data for one connector

Replace “which connector deserves review first?” with **“For this connector version, which required delivery modes have complete evidence, which cross the declared audience, and which legitimate modes regress after the correction?”**

The smallest useful population consists of actual versioned evaluation events across the owner's required delivery modes and recipient policies, with the original and corrected versions when a real defect exists. A declared case inventory distinguishes a missing run from a passing run. Evidence may include report type, approved-recipient/policy revision, observed recipient classification, delivery result and case identity. It must describe the lab's observations, not an imaginary fleet or customer exposure.

The query changes the release-review state when real evidence arrives: a necessary delivery mode is incomplete, a demonstrated audience check fails, or the corrected version retains all required legitimate results. The same query identifies the exact unresolved modes for the hosted reviewer. Disclosed diverse synthetic historical/replay cohorts can measure scale and query latency, but they do not create additional vulnerabilities or establish the active release's outcome. Keep active-case results separate.

A tiny acceptance matrix does not intrinsically need ClickHouse. Its companion prize case becomes persuasive only if continuous, versioned complete-run and comparison queries over a meaningful measured workload resolve that uncertainty visibly. If the day produces only a handful of rows and a storage count, remove the ClickHouse award claim rather than invent scale or customers. This uses the current scale/latency/insight criteria without assuming an official row-count floor. [Analytical-use standard](../competition-and-eligibility.md#logging-only-clickhouse-versus-an-analytical-detector-driving-guild).

## Useful hosted Guild work beyond “hold”

Give the real Guild reviewer the current query result, declared delivery requirements and evidence links. Its bounded job is to read the actual connector source/configuration through an existing repository tool, relate the unresolved delivery modes to that source, and produce an **actionable release change request** in the one owned repository after owner approval.

The artifact should identify the affected recipient/field or delivery mode, distinguish failed checks from missing checks, attach the actual evidence, and request the smallest source/configuration change or additional verification needed. It can take a different useful action when the evidence differs: an incomplete mode produces a targeted verification request; a real audience failure produces a grounded correction request. It does not merely repeat a predetermined verdict or claim authority to invent passing evidence.

The sponsor moment is a genuine hosted source read followed by a useful repository artifact, with its actual session and tool events inspectable. The deterministic acceptance contract retains control of pass/fail. If hosted access fails or the artifact adds no case-specific value, remove Guild from the required demonstration and keep the same release card with a labelled human review. [Guild capability/access sources](../clickhouse-guild.md).

## Ranked product judgment: A, B, C

1. **A — Code Vaccine.** Its best product advantage is a repeatable admission decision: a disclosed replay of the original bad change is rejected, while the verified version and legitimate feature behavior survive. Its Guardian-supported finding path is better established than C's outbound-transition path. The familiar JWT issue still needs an interesting genuine consequence; no finding currently exists. Its ClickHouse workload and hosted review must earn their place. A is first as the current five-hour product plan, conditional on the authentic input appearing promptly.
2. **B — ScopeWatch.** Cumulative read budgets across sessions have the most natural analytical question of the three, and selective control of a particular agent/credential is a clear operational decision. But events persist at turn end: it can assess completed-turn activity and constrain subsequent activity, not promise prevention of the first excessive read within that turn. The actual hosted investigation and operator-approved selective DENY remain unrun. B could become first if its trustworthy collection and next-turn enforcement work early while A lacks a qualifying finding.
3. **C — ToolTrust.** One connector release is a concrete user job and the same core survives failed discovery. Nevertheless, its flagship finding has more coverage/attribution uncertainty, and its analytics are less naturally necessary than B's cumulative budget. The narrower revised product is feasible to present, but these weaknesses remain after removing fleet ranking. C moves ahead only when the actual connector case and its useful hosted/analytical work are established while the alternatives remain hypothetical.

This ordering is a product judgment, **not prize odds or official scoring**. A or C without a genuine eligible finding cannot claim the Semgrep finding award. B need not invent a source defect to remain a useful runtime product. For all three, historical integration depth is not a proven winning predictor. The equal $1,000 top monetary awards, conditional stacking and unpriced Pi award make completed eligible evidence more important than preserving three logos.
