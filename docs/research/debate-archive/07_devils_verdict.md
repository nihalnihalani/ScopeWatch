# Independent final verdict: choose ScopeWatch

Prepared 9 October 2026 after reading the full [Code Vaccine brief and rebuttal](01_semgrep_advocate.md), [ScopeWatch brief and rebuttal](02_runtime_advocate.md), [ToolTrust brief](04_alternative_advocate.md), [ToolTrust cross-response](06_cross_rebuttals_tooltrust.md), and the [fresh permissions pulse](05_fresh_permissions_pulse.md). Research and product/prize planning only; no implementation, generated finding, operational test, hosted run or benchmark was performed. Akash excluded.

## Decision

**Build B, ScopeWatch: review cumulative credential use across completed agent sessions, create a grounded hosted investigation, and let the operator contain that workload while another authorized workload keeps working.**

Use a deliberately narrow minimum metric: **native approved integration-operation decision events against an operator-declared rolling approval budget**. This corrects B's weakest semantic assumption. A Guild `ALLOW` event proves a permission decision; it does not prove the downstream read succeeded, an object was returned, or bytes left a system. Calling the metric “documents read” or “data exfiltrated” would make the central evidence false. Successful-read/object/byte analytics require separately matched actual outcome receipts and are outside this minimum contract. [Native event schema and timing](https://docs.guild.ai/api-reference/sessions/fetch-session-events); saved primary capture: `.firecrawl/runtime-guild-events.md`.

The one user is an operator running hosted workloads with a shared integration credential. The one decision is: **“This workload exceeded the permission-use budget I assigned it; should I block its subsequent relevant operations while retaining the approved workload?”** The rule is a predeclared operational limit, not an accusation of malicious intent. A disclosed deterministic lab scenario suffices. Do not rely on persuading an LLM to behave badly or on discovering a new source vulnerability.

This is my product-planning judgment, not an official score, win probability or expected-value calculation. **ScopeWatch is first on the evidence currently available.** A real extraordinary source finding could have favored A or C, but none exists in this research. I am selecting one product now, rather than asking the team to build three proposals and discover the winner afterward.

ScopeWatch's aggregate is the actual problem. Several individually permitted decisions in separate sessions do not reveal the rolling workload total when considered separately. ClickHouse resolves that cumulative question and returns the contributing sessions. Guild supplies the hosted workloads, native permission decisions, held credentials, investigator and proposed enforcement surface. Their contributions belong to the same operator outcome.

**A, Code Vaccine, answered the no-finding challenge well:** its same incident-export app can retain a fixed authorization contract and admit a complete successful feature version while holding an incomplete evaluation. Its disclosed rollback challenge avoids needing a second accidental regression. However, without an authentic original finding, its signature vaccine story becomes an ordinary contract gate; the Semgrep award remains unavailable. With one app and a small acceptance matrix, ClickHouse is useful storage/completeness computation but less naturally necessary. Recurrence analytics and a future-change decision also add work after discovery, consequence confirmation and repair. Existing Pi, Semgrep and Snyk products already cover recurrence guardrails, agentic analysis and independent validation. [A's concessions](01_semgrep_advocate.md); [finding audit](../finding-feasibility.md); [Pi case study](https://www.pi.security/customers/lemonade).

**C, ToolTrust, has the clearest potential finding consequence:** a generated connector delivering a synthetic report beyond its approved recipient is easy to understand. The revised one-release card and missing-check treatment are coherent and survive no finding. But C conceded its double gate: the actual source defect must match an accepted stock CLI scan, and separate observation must establish the advertised recipient consequence. The inspected outbound rules are outside Guardian-default and do not establish the destination transition themselves. Removing invented fleet ranking was correct, but leaves another small acceptance matrix with optional database value. A vivid hypothesis cannot be counted as eligible finding evidence. [C's cross-response](06_cross_rebuttals_tooltrust.md); [exact audited rule limits](../finding-feasibility.md); [Flask stock rule](https://semgrep.dev/c/r/python.flask.security.injection.ssrf-requests.ssrf-requests).

**B has substantial remaining risks.** The native schema may not populate the chosen operation/identity fields in the event account. The documented scoped DENY may fail to act as expected. Shared credentials, actor mapping and initial allow-all policies can undermine an apparently selective setup. The investigator can become a decorative summary of an already deterministic count. A tiny native trace with a huge synthetic background is still only a lab control demonstration. These are first-hour proof obligations, not solved research facts. [Guild policies](https://docs.guild.ai/platform/credential-policies); [research availability audit](../clickhouse-guild.md).

B also gives up a mandatory third monetary track. That is a real opportunity cost. My reason for accepting it is that the other two proposals currently owe an authentic centerpiece finding *and* must make analytics and hosted review substantive. B spends that discovery-dependent critical path on two direct sponsor proofs. Without probabilities or entrant data, this is a scope/evidence choice; it is not a claim that $2,000 has a larger expected value than $3,000.

## Fixed minimum product contract

1. **Declare the policy before the scenario.** A server-owned versioned manifest names the launched workload, installed agent/workspace, credential, relevant operation family, rolling interval, approval-event allowance and preapproved bulk-work allowance. The operator owns this record. An agent's supplied identity or purpose string cannot change its budget.
2. **Collect native evidence from completed turns.** Preserve unique event ID, native task/session links, operation, decision, credential where supplied, timestamp and authenticated workload binding. Use the platform/task metadata plus the controller's own launch record for actor mapping. Unknown mapping or incomplete ingestion yields “incomplete evidence,” not an empty safe total. Deduplicate event IDs.
3. **Query cumulative use in ClickHouse.** Count the exact native ALLOW decision events defined by the manifest across contributing sessions. Apply each workload's own allowance and explicit exceptions. Return the budget version, count, interval and session evidence; do not invent unique-object or byte counts. State which native timestamp defines the window.
4. **Run a genuine Guild investigator.** Give it the bounded actual query result and manifest/evidence links. It reads relevant context and creates a specific reviewable incident record in one owned repository through a platform-held credential. It must distinguish an approved bulk workflow from a budget breach and propose the narrow operation/agent scope. The record and session/tool events are inspectable. The count remains deterministic; the investigator cannot edit the manifest, alter evidence, or authorize itself.
5. **Apply an operator-approved scoped DENY and verify residual access.** Demonstrate a real subsequent denied operation for the contained workload and a real successful authorized operation for the control workload using the shared credential. Save the policy identifier/scope, actual denial event and response evidence. A recommendation, local pause flag or disconnected association is not selective enforcement proof.

Guild documents agent/workspace/resource scopes and DENY precedence, but the research did not exercise them. A new credential's allow-all rule must be handled accurately before claiming a restricted setup. Disconnecting a CONNECTED association may delete the credential, so it is not a substitute for selective denial. [Credential policies](https://docs.guild.ai/platform/credential-policies); [disconnect endpoint](https://docs.guild.ai/api-reference/workspaces/disconnect-a-credential-association-from-a-workspace-agent).

**Timing is part of the contract:** events persist when the agent turn finishes. The minimum detects completed-turn activity and constrains subsequent relevant actions. It cannot honestly claim to stop the first excess operation during the same turn or to prevent the demonstrated aggregate from occurring. Include persistence/ingestion delay in the end-to-end timing, separately from SQL latency. [Official session-events documentation](https://docs.guild.ai/api-reference/sessions/fetch-session-events).

## Semantic controls required for an honest demo

The strongest positive control is a genuine authorized workflow continuing **after** the policy change. It must return the expected owned synthetic object/result, not merely an ALLOW decision or a familiar cached UI answer. The contained workload's repeated relevant operation must show the native denial and lack a fresh successful result. If both workloads fail, selective containment failed.

Include an approved bulk workload with a higher declared allowance. Its raw count can exceed the contained workload's count while staying compliant with its own policy. This proves the query uses declared context rather than equating volume with maliciousness. Include a replayed duplicate event and show that it does not create extra use or a second case.

Explicitly retain an ALLOW-followed-by-failed-execution example in the evidence explanation: it can count toward this **approval-event** budget because that is the predeclared metric, but it cannot be reported as a successful read or disclosure. If successful-read analytics are added later, that example must be excluded from the successful-delivery count. This prevents a quiet semantic upgrade of the headline.

These phases total 300 minutes. For four people, assign: Guild/native enforcement; ingestion/query; manifest/scenario/provenance; product/evidence/demo. With two people, keep one integration, two workload identities and one investigator; use existing supported tools and avoid a new remotely deployed evidence API. The genuine system-of-record issue is sufficient; no autonomous policy editing, agent swarm, generalized sandbox, patch factory, new UDF or elaborate observability deployment is required.

If the first-45-minute native enforcement proof fails, ask the sponsor mentor once and simplify within the same integration. Do not count selective containment or its Guild proof as complete until it works. A monitoring/review prototype remains the same product but has a materially weaker outcome; the final submission must say so. Likewise, no real hosted issue means no claim of completed hosted action. These are honest limitations, not permission to disguise a failed dependency or start A/C late.

For scale, the minimum authentic case is several completed sessions and the retained control workflow. A separate replay corpus should vary workload/credential/session/operation/time/allowance/decision cohorts and include late/duplicate records. It is a latency/correctness workload, not a customer fleet or millions of discoveries. Display actual stored rows, rows read, query p50/p95 over a stated bounded sample and database conditions, plus persistence-to-alert and policy-change-to-observed-denial timings. There is no verified million- or ten-million-row award floor. Plain MergeTree and a parameterized query suffice; changing manifest context belongs at query time if an incremental rollup is used. ## Novelty and historical lesson

Market novelty is weak for all three. WorkOS's 30 September checklist already recommends agent/session/tool/tenant budgets, circuit breakers and anomalous read-volume alerts. NVIDIA's 28 September platform already describes independent agent monitoring and policy containment. ScopeWatch should claim a compact working operator control with attributable evidence, not a new security category. These are first-party vendor statements, not independent proof of effectiveness or demand. The 58-record pulse is bounded selected discussion, not 58 confirmed incidents. [WorkOS checklist](https://workos.com/blog/ai-agent-permissions-checklist); [NVIDIA reference architecture](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-open-agent-safety-platform-a-reference-for-continuous-in-silicon-agent-monitoring); [pulse and limits](05_fresh_permissions_pulse.md).

The most useful winner lesson is **make the sponsor's causal contribution inspectable**. Sponsor-confirmed Rokko first place used analytics in an action loop. DailyGate's verified Guild award membership supports clearly different tool grants, while its scripted live panel is a warning to show real sessions. Argus's real secretless ticketing step is a useful model, but its local “Guild pause” should not be copied as platform enforcement. [Verified matrix](../all-relevant-winners.md); [corrected code forensics](../clickhouse-guild.md); [official ClickHouse recap](https://clickhouse.com/blog/aws-mcp-hackathon-san-francisco).

Do not turn that lesson into a winning formula: IncidentSherpa won Senso while losing ClickHouse/Guild despite meaningful analytics, and LicenseTrace won ClickHouse with a thin inspected integration. Selected archives, uncertain ranks, code-version differences and absent judge scores prevent causal conclusions or win odds. ScopeWatch is selected for its coherent current contract and two substantive sponsor roles, with a truthful optional third finding opportunity—not because a historical pattern guarantees an award. [Counterexamples and limitations](../competition-and-eligibility.md).

**Final product commitment: ScopeWatch, one cross-session permission-use budget, one hosted investigation record, one operator-approved selective subsequent-operation control, and one genuinely successful retained workload.**
